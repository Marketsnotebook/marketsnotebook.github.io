Forex Trading Sessions: Why Liquidity Varies Through the Day

October 11, 2026 · Markets Notebook

Retail traders often wonder why the cost to trade the same currency pair can vary significantly within hours. The answer lies in the rotation of the world’s major market centres, how liquidity ebbs and flows, and how brokers translate those movements into spreads and execution. Understanding session timing, overlaps, and rollover hours helps traders estimate transaction costs and manage slippage risk.

The Four Major Forex Trading Sessions

Sydney – The market opens in Sydney around 10:00 pm UTC (5:00 pm EST). With European and North-American markets closed, trading volume is generally low. Liquidity concentrates in the AUD, NZD, and cross-rates involving these currencies. Typical spreads for AUD/USD can widen to 2.5–4.0 pips on a standard ECN broker, compared to sub-1 pip spreads later in the day.

Tokyo – The Tokyo session begins at 12:00 am UTC (7:00 pm EST) and ends at 9:00 am UTC (4:00 am EST). As the first major Asian hub, it brings higher participation from Japanese banks and corporations. Activity spikes for JPY-linked pairs such as USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, and AUD/JPY. Spreads tighten relative to Sydney but remain above London-time averages, often 1.5–2.5 pips for USD/JPY.

London – Opening at 8:00 am UTC (3:00 am EST), London is the largest and most liquid FX centre. The market processes the majority of interbank trades, and virtually all major currency pairs see their narrowest spreads. For EUR/USD, a typical ECN spread can be 0.5–1.0 pips during the London open, with sufficient depth of market to absorb several-hundred-lot orders without noticeable price impact.

New York – The New York session overlaps with London from 13:00 UTC (8:00 am EST) until 22:00 UTC (5:00 pm EST). Its volume is dominated by USD-centric pairs. Benefiting from London liquidity, the first few hours of the New York session often retain the tight spreads seen in London, especially for USD-based crosses like GBP/USD and EUR/USD. As the New York close approaches, spreads may widen modestly, typically to 1.2–2.0 pips for EUR/USD.

Key Session Overlaps and Their Impact

  • London/New York overlap (approx. 8 am – 12 pm EST) – This four-hour window concentrates the deepest liquidity pool. Order books are densely populated, reducing the bid-ask gap and improving fill probability. For a trader using a 0.1 lot (10 k) position on EUR/USD, the cost difference between a 0.5-pip spread at 9 am EST and a 1.2-pip spread at 5 pm EST can result in a 2 pip (≈ $2) increase in slippage cost per trade.

  • Sydney/Tokyo overlap (approx. 5 am – 7 am EST) – Liquidity improves for AUD/JPY and NZD/JPY, but overall market depth remains modest. Spreads typically narrow by 0.3–0.5 pips compared to the isolated Sydney session, offering a small but measurable cost advantage for traders focused on those crosses.

  • London/Tokyo overlap (approx. 12 am – 2 am EST) – This short window provides a modest boost to JPY pair liquidity, but the effect is limited compared to the London/New York overlap. Spreads for USD/JPY may tighten from 2.0 pips to around 1.5 pips.

  • Increased volume during overlaps generally leads to better execution because market makers have more counterparties to match. Conversely, when a session runs alone, order flow can be thin, leading to larger gaps between the best bid and ask and a higher likelihood of price jumps.

Traders can align their preferred currency pairs with sessions offering the most favourable cost structure. A EUR-focused swing trader might schedule entries around the London open, while a JPY day-trader could target the Tokyo session and its brief overlap with London.

Understanding Rollover Hour and Spread Widening

The rollover hour is the 60-minute period when positions held past the broker’s daily cut-off (usually 5:00 pm EST for most retail platforms) receive their overnight swap adjustment. During this window:

  1. Liquidity contracts – Many institutions reduce exposure to avoid carrying positions overnight, thinning the order book.
  2. Volatility can spike – News releases timed for the start of a new trading day (e.g., U.S. macro data at 8:30 am EST) often cause rapid price moves.
  3. Brokers widen spreads – To mitigate heightened risk, brokers may increase the quoted spread by 0.5–2.0 pips, depending on the pair and the broker’s liquidity provider. For less liquid instruments like exotic crosses (e.g., USD/TRY), the spread can double or triple for the rollover minute.

Because spread widening is temporary, a trader holding a position through rollover should account for the extra cost in trade-size calculations. For instance, a scalper using a 0.01 lot (1 k) on EUR/USD with a typical 0.5-pip spread might see the spread rise to 1.5 pips for one minute. This costs roughly $0.10 in slippage—significant when the strategy relies on sub-1-pip edges.

How Session Dynamics Affect Spreads

Sydney (open) 2.0-3.0 pips 2.5-3.5 pips Low
Tokyo (peak) 1.2-1.8 pips 1.5-2.0 pips Moderate
London (open) 0.5-1.0 pips 0.8-1.2 pips High
London/New York overlap 0.4-0.9 pips 0.7-1.1 pips Highest
New York (close) 1.0-1.5 pips 1.2-1.8 pips Moderate-Low

The table illustrates that spreads are not static; they react to the underlying interbank market. Brokers typically apply a fixed markup to the raw interbank spread. During high-liquidity periods, the markup may be as low as 0.1 pips, while during low-liquidity windows, it can rise to 0.5 pips or more. Consequently, a trader planning to open a 0.5 lot position on EUR/USD at 9 am EST (London open) can expect a quoted spread of about 0.8 pips. The same trade at 4 pm EST (late New York) may face a spread of 1.3 pips, increasing the breakeven point by roughly $0.65.

When evaluating a broker, monitor spread behavior across at least a full 24-hour cycle. A practical test involves placing a series of micro-lot market orders on a liquid pair (e.g., EUR/USD) every hour for a week and recording the quoted spread. The observed range provides a realistic picture of the broker’s cost structure beyond the “average spread” advertised on their website.

Execution Quality and Slippage

Slippage occurs when an order is filled at a price different from the requested price. It is quantified in pips; 0.2-pip slippage on a 0.1 lot EUR/USD trade results in a $2 cost. Two factors dominate slippage risk:

  1. Liquidity at the moment of execution – In thin markets (Sydney open, late New York), a 100,000 unit order can move the price several pips before the broker can locate a counter-party.
  2. Broker execution technology – Brokers that route orders directly to multiple ECNs, use smart-order routing, or co-locate servers near liquidity providers tend to achieve higher fill rates with minimal slippage. For example, a broker reporting a “fill ratio” of 98% on market orders during the London/New York overlap suggests that only 2% of orders experience slippage beyond the quoted spread.

Traders can mitigate slippage by:

  • Using limit orders instead of market orders during low-liquidity periods.
  • Reducing order size to stay within the typical depth of market (e.g., < 0.2 lot during Sydney).
  • Avoiding the rollover hour unless the strategy explicitly incorporates overnight positions.

Understanding session timing enables traders to align order placement with periods where the broker’s execution engine is most likely to deliver the expected price.

Key Takeaways

  • Liquidity fluctuates significantly between the Sydney, Tokyo, London, and New York trading sessions.
  • Session overlaps, particularly London and New York, offer the highest liquidity and typically the tightest spreads.
  • Be aware of potential spread widening and increased slippage risk during low-liquidity periods and rollover hour.
  • Understanding session dynamics helps traders choose optimal trading times and evaluate broker execution quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

When are forex spreads usually tightest?

Spreads are typically tightest during the London/New York overlap when liquidity is highest; for major pairs, the quoted spread can be under 1 pip on most ECN-style brokers.

Does broker choice matter for session spreads?

Yes, brokers with direct market access or better liquidity providers may offer more competitive spreads throughout the day. Execution speed and the degree of markup applied to the interbank spread vary between brokers, influencing the cost at any given session.

What is the ‘rollover hour’?

It’s the time when overnight interest charges (swaps) are applied to positions held open past midnight, often with wider spreads. The hour generally coincides with the broker’s daily cut-off (commonly 5:00 pm EST) and can see reduced liquidity and heightened volatility.
